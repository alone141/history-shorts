# Fact check: Colosseum holes film (hostile read, 16 Nov 2026)

Read directly: the Middleton p.87 and Suaresius p.4 image files; Met API (curl); archive.org metadata and OCR of bub_gb_q1SlcBKcJjgC; Art of Making 411; the-colosseum.net/en/middle-ages. NOT read (paywalled/lending): Lancaster, Claridge, Rea, Parco pages on clamps. Verdicts on "modern consensus" rest on the Art of Making (King's College London) and the-colosseum.net only.

## Met records (API, all isPublicDomain true)
- 287727: Jane Martha St. John, "The Colosseum", 1856, albumen silver print, 2005.100.382 (68). Correct.
- 306254: Calvert Richard Jones, "67. Colosseum, Rome, Second View", May 1846, salted paper print, 2013.159.34. Correct.
- 360270: Piranesi, "Veduta dell'Anfiteatro Flavio detto il Colosseo", 1776, etching, 59.570.426. Correct.

## Lines
1. **True but challengeable.** Some holes are original (lifting holes: Manieri Elia via research.md; marble-fixture clamp sockets inside), and weathering enlarges pits. Art of Making: "The pock-marked facade ... is a result of the removal of iron clamps" (https://artofmaking.ac.uk/explore/sources/411). Replace: "The Colosseum is full of holes, and most were made by people."
2. **True.** Met record above.
3. **True but challengeable.** A dark dot in an 1856 print can be a weathering pit, a shadow, or a lifting hole. Replace: "Look at the piers. The dark dots are holes." (or "Look at the piers. Those dark dots are holes.")
4. **Misleading as worded.** Date: archive.org item q1SlcBKcJjgC is dated 1651; the Lyon reprint (DYt4WNOy6wEC) is 1652. I could not read a title-page imprint in the OCR, so "1651" is defensible only as the first edition. Author: archive creator "Joseph Marie Suares", bishop of Vaison (Suaresius): right. Page 4 as shown (read at full size) has barbarians ("feritatem Barbarorum", quoting Donatus) and market stalls ("in nundinis", "perticas ... tabernarum"), and the page ends by calling the holes "signa nundinantis industriae, quam Barbariae saevientis" (signs of market trade rather than raging barbarism). It has NO earthquake: that is page 2 ("terraemotu ... plane ridiculum", OCR line ~2782 area). Also he rejects barbarians and the earthquake and prefers stalls on p.4, and by p.9 lists metal-robbing among six causes. "Listed the suspects" is fair for the book, not for the page shown. Replace: "In 1651, a bishop's book weighed barbarians, an earthquake, market stalls." and show p.2 or p.4 honestly, or say "two of them are on this page".
5. **True but challengeable.** Middleton p.87, body: "The whole of the exterior of the Colosseum is of massive travertine, in very large blocks, carefully jointed, set without mortar, and each clamped to the next with heavy iron clamps, run with lead." Footnote 3: "The numerous holes, which disfigure the arcades, were made during the early Middle Ages in attempts to extract the then valuable iron of the clamps; the amount of trouble taken in cutting through the hard travertine to extract each clamp shows that labour must then have been worth but very little, or iron very scarce." Modern: Art of Making says "iron clamps" and "post-Roman"; I found no analysis of surviving metal, and "set in lead" rests on Manieri Elia's thesis. "Real answer" overreaches (1892, no evidence on page; "no mortar" is disputed). Replace: "The usual answer is on this page: iron clamps, set in lead."
6. **True but challengeable.** Clamps joined blocks; "lock" is rhetoric. Replace: "The Romans used them to tie the stone blocks together."
7. **True but challengeable.** "After Rome fell": Middleton says "early Middle Ages"; Art of Making says "post-Roman"; the-colosseum.net says "probably during the Gothic War" (535-553), hedged. No dated excavation evidence. "Hole by hole" is fair (Middleton: effort per clamp). Replace: "In the centuries after Rome fell, people dug the metal out, clamp by clamp."
8. **Misleading.** Art of Making: "Approximately 300 tonnes of metal, it has been estimated, was used in the construction." It is metal used, unattributed, not metal removed. "Held" is fair; "walls" is loose. Replace: "Estimates say the building used about 300 tons of metal."
9. **Rhetoric, with a basis.** Art of Making: "In the post-Roman period these clamps were more valuable when melted down as raw materials than they were holding these blocks in place." "More than the monument" is an overstatement. Acceptable only softened. Replace: "To some, the metal was worth more than the stone."

Caption plate: "about 300 tons of metal (estimate)" should add "used in building". "IRON CLAMPS ROBBED AFTER ANTIQUITY" is fine. Source line fine.
