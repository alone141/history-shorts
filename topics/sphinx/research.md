# Research: "Napoleon didn't shoot off the Sphinx's nose"

Source research for a 30-second film. Checked 14 November 2026. Verdicts: verified (read in the primary source or two independent sources), uncertain, false. Page coordinates are pixels of the upright, full-resolution scan (plates: after rotating the stored jp2 90 degrees clockwise).

## 1. Norden: what he drew, when, and when it was printed

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Norden drew the Sphinx in 1737 | **verified** (engraved legend) | Plate XLV, "Tête colossale du Sphynx, avec les trois Pyramides", carries the engraved line "F. L. Norden del.; 1737" (read at 4x; `del.` = delineavit, drew). Vol. 2 scan 0096. |
| Norden was in Egypt 1737-38; at Cairo 7 July to 17/18 Nov 1737 | **verified** | Vol. 1 preface: arrived Cairo "le 7. de Juillet", "Obligé par une grande maladie ... d'y faire un séjour de plus de quatre mois ... d'aller voir les Pyramides situées à quelque distance de cette Capitale. Etant enfin parvenu le 17. de Novembre à s'y embarquer sur le Nil"; returned "le 6. de Janvier 1738", landed 21 Feb 1738, left Alexandria "sur la fin du Mai". Norden's own letter says "j'y arrivai le 7. de Juillet 1737.; & je n'en partis que le 18. de Novembre" (vol. 1, Lettre, OCR line 7862). The exact day he drew the Sphinx is **not stated** in what I read. Safe: "in 1737". |
| Plates engraved by Marcus Tuscher | **verified** | Preface: "les Desseins fussent gravés par le célèbre Marc Tuscher Nurenbergeois"; "À sa mort toutes les Planches se trouvèrent gravées, à l'exception de la dernière ou 159e". Plate XLV: "M. Tuscher Hafniae fec. 1743" (Hafniae = Copenhagen). Plate XLVI: "M. Tuscher Sculp. 1744". Plate XLVII: "Norden & Tuscher f." (no year). |
| Published 1755 | **verified** | Title page vol. 1: "A COPENHAGUE, DE L'IMPRIMERIE DE LA MAISON ROYALE DES ORPHELINS. MDCCLV." Same imprint in vol. 2 (Internet Archive metadata date 1755). |
| Norden died before publication | **verified** | Preface: "La mort nous l'enleva enfin à Paris le 22. Sept. 1742". Published 13 years after his death. He never saw the plates printed. |
| Plate numbers: XLV (head with the three pyramids), XLVI (head from the front), XLVII (profile) | **verified** | Table des planches, vol. 1: "XLV. Tête Colossale du Sphinx, avec les trois Pyramides. XLVI. Tête Colossale du Sphinx, vuë en face, au devant de la seconde Pyramide. XLVII. Profil de la Tête Colossale du Sphinx." Printed plate numbers read XLV, XLVI, XLVII at top right of each plate. Plate title as engraved on XLVII: "Profil de la tête colossale du Sphinx." |
| The profile (XLVII) shows the nose gone | **verified by eye** | At 4x the face ends in a flat, broken stump between the eye and the lip. A faint, thin outline drawn beyond the stump suggests where a nose would have been (see hostile list, no. 2). Plate XLVI front view shows a hollow, damaged nose; XLV is small and less clear. |
| Norden's own text mentions the damage | **verified** | Vol. 1, printed p. 85: "on arrive au Sphinx, dont on admire la grandeur énorme, en concevant une forte d'indignation pour ceux, qui ont eu la brutalité de maltraiter étrangement son nez." Not Norden's drawing-date text: impersonal "on" in an editorial chapter (see hostile list, no. 4). |

Editions: 1755 is the first edition, in French, two volumes, folio. An English translation by Peter Templeman followed in 1757 ("Travels in Egypt and Nubia"); some websites mistakenly date the plates 1757. Say "1755" for the original.

Safest spoken phrases: "drawn in 1737, before Napoleon was born", "printed in 1755, 43 years before his army reached Giza", "over 60 years before". The two year gaps depend on the date, so name the date.

## 3. Al-Maqrizi and Sa'im al-Dahr

**Primary text, read in Arabic** (Madbouli 1998 ed., vol. 1, section "ذكر الصنم الذي يقال له أبو الهول", Internet Archive `02_20230830_202308`, `01_djvu.txt` around OCR line 12382; same wording in the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya ed., `Al-Muawiz_Wa_Al-Aitebaar_Bi-Zikar_Al-Khatat_Wa-Al-Aasaar`):

> وفي زمننا كان شخص يعرف بالشيخ محمد صائم الدهر من جملة صوفية الخانقاه الصلاحية سعيد السعداء، قام في نحو من سنة ثمانين وسبعمائة لتغيير أشياء من المنكرات، وسار إلى الأهرام وشوه وجه أبي الهول وشعثه، فهو على ذلك إلى اليوم، ومن حينئذ غلب الرمل على أراض كثيرة من الجيزة ...

My translation: "In our time there was a man known as Shaykh Muhammad Sa'im al-Dahr, one of the Sufis of the Salahiyya khanqah, Sa'id al-Su'ada. About the year 780 [AH] he set out to change some wrongs, went to the Pyramids, and disfigured the face of Abu al-Hawl and marred it; it has been so to this day. From then the sand overcame much land in Giza..."

Points that matter:
- Maqrizi says **"in about the year 780"** (AH 780 = 2 May 1378 to 20 Apr 1379), "about" (نحو), not an exact date.
- He says **"disfigured the face"** (شوه وجه). The passage as I read it **does not say "nose"**.
- Maqrizi (died 1442) writes "in our time" but he was born about 1364; he is relating something about 1378, and does not say where he heard it. A later written account, not proof.

**Scholarly source that names the nose:** Ulrich Haarmann, "Die Sphinx. Synkretistische Volksreligiosität im spätmittelalterlichen islamischen Ägypten", *Saeculum* 24 (1978), pp. 367-384, here p. 378 (read on the author's offprint, Freiburg FreiDok https://freidok.uni-freiburg.de/dnb/download/4619):

> "In demselben Jahr 1378 erwarb ein frommer Scheich aus dem ältesten und angesehensten Kairoer Derwischkloster, ein gewisser Ṣā'im ad-dahr, zu deutsch: „jemand, der die ganze Zeit fastet", traurige Berühmtheit. ... Jetzt richtete er seinen ikonoklastischen Zorn gegen die Sphinx, ... und beschädigte ihre Nase und ihre Ohren bzw. was davon noch übriggeblieben war."

Haarmann's note 46 cites al-Maqrizi, *Ḥiṭaṭ* (his note 10) p. 157; Ibn 'Abd as-Salam (MS fol. 52a, citing ar-Rashidi "einem Zeitgenossen des Ereignisses", a contemporary of the event); Gelalzade Salih Celebi, *Tarih-i gedid-i Misir*. So Haarmann reads the nose and ears from the wider tradition, not from Maqrizi's words alone. His 1980 article, "Regional sentiment in medieval Islamic Egypt", *BSOAS* 43(1), concerns Egyptian regional identity and cites the Sphinx among Egypt's wonders; **I could not read it** and did not find the nose passage there. Cite the 1978 article for the incident.

Also cited for the same story (via Wikipedia footnotes; **not read by me**): Okasha El-Daly, *Egyptology: The Missing Millennium* (Routledge/UCL Press, 2005), pp. 88-89 (Google Books PA88/PA89 links); Lowry, Toorawa & Rowson (eds), *Arabic Humanities, Islamic Thought* (Brill, 2017), pp. 263-264. Hawass: I found no quotable passage; do not cite him.

**How secure:** the incident is a single Mamluk-era report, with a few later chroniclers repeating it. It is the earliest *named* explanation, but it is not proof that al-Dahr broke the nose, and some popular sites misdate it ("12th century"). Lehner states a conflicting view (section 4).

## 4. Mark Lehner: deliberately broken

- NOVA, "Riddles of the Sphinx" (PBS, first aired 19 Jan 2010), transcript https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/transcripts/3703_sphinx.html. Narrator: "There are stories that it was shot off by Napoleon's soldiers using it for target practice, but they turn out to be false." Lehner: "About 300 years before Napoleon, an Arab historian already mentions that the nose is gone." "I think there is good evidence that it was snapped off intentionally." "There's a deep wedge down the bridge and there's another groove down the Sphinx's left nostril." "I think somebody might have pounded in these metal wedges and snapped it off to the south." **verified** (transcript).
- Lehner's 1991 Harvard dissertation (*Archaeology of an Image: The Great Sphinx of Giza*), quoted on the English Wikipedia talk page (I could not open the PDF): "It is clear that the nose was intentionally broken off, most likely by long chisels or wedges". **uncertain** (second-hand quote, no page).
- The Wikipedia article says "marks made by long rods or chisels hammered into the face: one at the bridge of the nose, and another below the nostril", citing Lehner 1997, *The Complete Pyramids*, p. 11. I could not open that book; the same talk page quotes it as "it is not known when or by whom the Sphinx's nose was broken". So: the "one down from the bridge, one under the nostril" wording is **uncertain**; Lehner's own spoken wording is the NOVA one above (wedge down the bridge, groove down the left nostril, snapped off to the south).
- "Lehner dated it to the 3rd to 10th centuries AD" appears on many websites. A Wikipedia editor disputes that attribution. **Do not use.**
- Lehner's "about 300 years before Napoleon" (c. 1500) does not match Maqrizi (died 1442, writing about 1378); he is speaking loosely. **Do not repeat.**

## 5. Earlier pictures that show a nose

| Picture | Shows nose? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pococke, *A Description of the East*, vol. 1 (London, 1743), plate of the Sphinx head, Observations on Egypt, near p. 44 | Wikipedia says yes | Pococke's own preface (vol. 1, OCR line 601) says the head of the sphinx is "from Le Brun, (though this is with some alterations, as to the ornaments of the head)". Le Brun = Cornelis de Bruijn. So it is a copy, not Pococke's own view, though he was at Giza in 1737 too (Wikipedia). Pococke's text (p. 44) measures the Sphinx and mentions no nose. **I did not see the plate**; "has a nose" rests on Wikipedia. |
| de Bruijn (Le Brun), *Voyage au Levant*, 1698 (Dutch 1698, French 1700) | per Wikipedia and popular sources, yes | Not checked here. |
| Sandys 1615, Helferich 1579 | popular sources say a female face with a nose | Not checked; weak sourcing. They are generally treated as imaginative. |

Also noteworthy: Wikipedia calls Norden's the earliest known drawing with the nose missing ("citation needed"). **Do not say "first".** Norden himself writes in the book that "ceux qui ont eu la brutalité" had damaged it, so he describes the damage as already done.

## 6. What a hostile commenter could say

1. **"Norden's plate came out after he died."** True, and the plates were engraved after his death too. Norden died 22 Sept 1742; plate XLV is engraved 1743, XLVI 1744 (by Tuscher, who also died before the book appeared). He never checked the engraving. Say "Norden's drawing, engraved after his death", not "Norden's print".
2. **Engraver's liberties.** In plate XLVII a very faint outline extends beyond the broken nose, as if a nose were sketched in lightly. Anyone zooming will see it. It does not change the fact that the solid drawing stops at the stump, but the film should not claim "no trace of a nose". Plates XLV and XLVI differ from XLVII in the treatment of the face; all show it damaged.
3. **"The 1755 book is not 1737."** Correct; the 1737 date is on plate XLV only. The 1755 date is on the title page. Use whichever the on-screen scan shows.
4. **Which words are Norden's?** The page 85 sentence is in the book's text, which the preface says the Danish society edited and put in order from his notes ("Les Mémoires demandoient plus d'application pour être rédigés et mis au net"). Say "the 1755 book says", not "Norden wrote in 1737".
5. **Maqrizi is later and says "face".** See section 3. Do not state "al-Dahr broke the nose in 1378" as fact.
6. **Pococke 1743 shows a nose.** A hostile commenter can post it. It is a copy of de Bruijn (Pococke says so). The film need not mention it.
7. **Other earlier pictures** (de Bruijn 1698, Sandys, Helferich) exist with noses; scholars treat them as inaccurate or imaginative, but I did not find a scholarly citation for that. Do not say "all earlier drawings are wrong".
8. **Did Napoleon's soldiers fire at it?** Absence of evidence. The honest claim is only: the nose was already missing before 1798 (two pictures and a book published 1755), so the damage was not done by his army. Napoleon's savants drew it in 1798-99 (not checked here). Do not claim Napoleon never visited the Sphinx; Gérôme's painting is an imagined scene.
9. **Gérôme's date.** The file I downloaded is tagged "1886" on Commons (source: a bare imgur link); another Commons file of the same composition (Yorck Project, 2048x1214) is tagged 1867-68. Treat the painting as "Gérôme, c. 1867-68" or just "Gérôme" unless the owner confirms 1886 from a museum record (Hearst Castle holds it).
10. **Ottoman/Arab 18th-century eyewitness.** Not looked for.

## 8. Do not say (not secure)

- "Al-Maqrizi says Sa'im al-Dahr broke the nose": he says "disfigured the face". Only Haarmann (1978) says nose and ears.
- "Sa'im al-Dahr did it in 1378" as fact: "about 780 AH", one later report.
- "Norden drew it in 1737 and published it in 1737/1757", or "Norden published": he died in 1742; it appeared in 1755.
- "Norden's is the first/earliest picture without a nose": unsourced.
- "Lehner dates the break to the 3rd-10th century", or "Lehner says about 1500".
- "No trace of a nose in Norden's profile": a faint outline exists.
- "The nose was shot off by cannon / Napoleon's soldiers never fired": say only what the dates show (the nose was missing before he was born, in a drawing made 32 years before).
- "Pococke's 1743 plate is an independent view": it is a copy of de Bruijn with changes.
- Any exact day for Norden's Giza visit.
