# FACTS: What did the Great Pyramid originally look like?

Every spoken and on-screen claim, its source, and the wording or the measurement
behind it. Quotations are kept to the words that carry the fact.

Status: written after a fact check in which each claim was sourced by one researcher
and then attacked by two hostile checkers, and revised after a review of the finished
film (14 November 2026); see "Refutation record" at the end. Not final until the film
is approved.

## Spoken (and shown as captions)

| # | Claim | Source | Wording or method |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | "What did the Great Pyramid originally look like?" | | The question. It is spoken and stays on screen. |
| 1 | "What stands today is a staircase: 203 courses of stone." | Petrie 1883, Plate VIII; Maragioglio and Rinaldi 1965 p. 16 | Plate VIII enters 203 course levels at the N.E. corner and 201 at the S.W. Petrie's text does not print the number; it is the count of the levels on his plate, and Maragioglio and Rinaldi cite it so ("The courses of the nucleus are now 203"). Others count differently: Goyon 200 to the flat top plus one ruined course; Kawae a 202nd layer; see "Known disagreements". "Today" rests on levels taken in 1880-82: Goyon found the level of the flat top unchanged since 1801 and its tallest block 1.12 m (Petrie's two top courses add to 1.115 m), and Kawae's survey still finds 18 stones above the flat top. Petrie, Smyth and Goyon all call the courses steps. "Stepped core" is avoided: in the literature it means an inner step pyramid, which is not shown for this pyramid. |
| 2 | "It ends in a flat top, about 11 metres across." | Petrie 1883 p. 43 | At the level of the flat top Petrie put the planes of the four sides 224·5 (N), 214·1 (E), 215·0 (S) and 217·6 (W) inches from the centre: 11.16 m north to south by 10.97 m east to west. Other measurers: Jomard 9.9 m, Smyth about 10.2 m, Vyse about 33 ft, Goyon about 10 by 10 m, the Giza 3D Survey 11.7 by 11.9 m (Kawae 2024). "About 11" follows Petrie, whose survey the model is built from, and lies inside that range. |
| 3 | "But it was finished smooth: cased in white limestone, rising to a point." | Petrie 1883 pp. 43-44, 144, 213; Lucas 1948 p. 69; Lehner 1997 pp. 108-109, 220-221; Dash 2015 p. 8; Tallet and Marouard 2014 p. 10 | White limestone: Petrie, "fine white Mokattam limestone like that on the Great Pyramid" (p. 144); Lehner, "fine white Turah limestone casing". It is taken to be from the Tura quarries across the Nile (Lucas, "almost certainly from the Tura quarries"; Dash; a logbook of Khufu's reign records Tura blocks shipped to the pyramid, "probably used for the external casing"). The film does not name the quarry. "Finished", not "built": the usual reconstruction is that the upper casing was laid with extra stock and dressed smooth from the top down at the end (Lehner pp. 220-221; Edwards 1961 pp. 224-225); Petrie held that the lowest blocks were dressed before they were set (in Edwards pp. 226-227). "Finished smooth" is true on either view. Two joints Petrie measured between casing blocks at the north base average a fiftieth of an inch (p. 44). "White" is the stone as dressed; Petrie notes that this limestone weathers to a brown tint (p. 54). "Polished" is not said: it has support (Herodotus 2.124, in translation; Vyse 1840 I p. 261) but the tool marks are of fine picking or adzing (Petrie p. 213). "To a point": see row 8; a pointed capstone is inferred from those that survive from other pyramids (row 5). |
| 4 | "Almost all of that casing was quarried away in the Middle Ages, much of it to build Cairo." | Lehner 1997 pp. 41, 109; Dash 2015 p. 8; Monnier 2022 p. 12; Saint voyage 1395; Abd al-Latif c. 1200 | Almost all: of the 921.44 m round the base, Lehner found casing blocks still in place along 54.44 m, "much of it badly worn"; Dash, "only 54 meters ... of casing stone in place, and much of that is badly damaged". What is left is part of the lowest course, about 1.5 m high: roughly a thousandth of the surface. In the Middle Ages: Monnier puts the dismantling from before the end of the 12th century to the 16th century at the latest. About 1200 Abd al-Latif described the two large pyramids in terms his translator took to mean they still had their casing, "at least in great part"; on 24 November 1395 French pilgrims watched masons on one of the Giza pyramids (the account does not say which) prising off the covering stones, of which, they wrote, most of the fine works of Cairo and Babylon (Old Cairo) were made. Much of it, not all: some stone went before and after (Lehner says stripping at Giza began as early as the New Kingdom; Petrie records villagers still taking casing fragments in his day). No year and no culprit is given: see "Not said". |
| 5 | "The top is gone too, and its own capstone has never been found." | Edwards 1961 p. 89; Petrie 1883 p. 43; Jánosi 1992 p. 301; Lehner 1997 pp. 222-223; Hawass 1997 p. 109; Rossi 1999 p. 221 | The top: Edwards, "About a dozen courses and the capstone, possibly made of granite, have been removed from the apex." By Petrie's figures 9.3 m is missing above the flat top, 8.2 m above the highest block on it. The light on screen during this line is that whole missing top (masonry, casing and capstone together), which is why the line names the top first; no capstone is drawn as a separate thing. The capstone: none has been published or identified. Jánosi calls the Dahshur capstone the only one preserved from a king's pyramid of the Old Kingdom; Lehner calls Khufu's small satellite pyramid "one of only two Old Kingdom pyramids for which the pyramidion (capstone) survives (the other is the North Pyramid at Dahshur)". "Its own" is there because of that satellite pyramid (G I-d): its limestone capstone was found in the 1990s near the Great Pyramid's south-east corner (Hawass 1997 says 1993; Hawass 2003 says 1991) and is sometimes reported as the Great Pyramid's. That a single capstone was set is the usual view (Edwards); Romer (2007) doubts it can be known. There is no evidence for gold on it: "There is no direct evidence that pyramidions were cased in the Fourth Dynasty" (Hawass 2003 p. 160). |

## The course table

`topics/giza/courses.json` holds the level of every course in inches above the pavement,
at the N.E. corner (203 values) and the S.W. corner (201 values).

- The plate was cut into enlarged tiles (`plate_tiles.py`). Every tile was read twice, independently. The two readings agreed on all but two numerals. The 36 rows on which they differed, or on which either reader was unsure, were read a third time.
- The plate checks itself in three ways, and `build_courses.py` applies all three: the strips of the fold-out overlap (19 courses were read from two scans); each course's line is drawn at 1/200 scale along the plate, so its position gives the level again to about two and a half inches; and each course's thickness is drawn as cross-lines at 1/10 scale, which the difference between one level and the next must meet to 0.6 inch. With the five readings below settled, there are no failures. Tried in the code review by changing one reading at a time, the checks catch an error of two inches or more nearly always and an error of one inch about three times in four.
- Five readings are settled against the drawing, each with its reason in `build_courses.py`. Two are slips on the plate itself: it prints 2337·7 and 2337·9 for course 70, which stands between 2209·9 and 2266·5 (the line's position and cross-lines give 2237·7 and 2237·9); and its brace for the S.W. second course reads 110·0 and 110·2 where Petrie's own text (p. 41) prints +111·2 and the cross-lines agree with the text. Two are blotted last digits (S.W. courses 116 and 132) fixed by the cross-lines to a tenth of an inch. None moves anything on screen.
- A tenth of an inch rests on the double reading alone.
- An independent transcription of the plate's N.E. column, found by the code review (https://cheops-pyramide.ch/khufu-pyramid/stonecourses-pyramid.html), agrees with this table on the thickness of 202 of the 203 courses to a millimetre. The one difference is course 166 (53.8 cm here, 65.5 cm there), and it is that table's slip: its column adds up to 5456.3 inches where Petrie's top level is 5451.8. The S.W. column was not compared.

## Modelling choices (not measurements)

- **Outline of the steps.** Each of the 201 courses up to the flat top is drawn as a rectangular slab with upright sides, its top edge on the plane Petrie fixed by eye over the prominent edges of the masonry on that side. At the pavement he put that plane on average 33·7 inches inside the casing's base line at the corners (Plate X gives 27·7 to 39·4 at the eight corner positions; the model uses 33·7 on all four sides). At the flat top he put it 224·5, 214·1, 215·0 and 217·6 inches from the centre (his stated errors: 0·7, 0·3, 0·4 and 1·0 inch). Between the two, each face is drawn as one flat plane, so the slabs are square at the bottom and 11.16 by 10.97 m at the top. These are Petrie's sight-planes, not a survey of every step edge.
- **The two courses above the flat top.** Petrie enters their levels (5430·4 and 5451·8 inches, N.E. only) and says nothing of their extent. Later observers describe a few blocks near the middle of the platform: Goyon one ruined course, its tallest block about a cubic metre; Kawae 18 stones of the 202nd layer against 86 exposed in the 201st. They are drawn as one low slab of about a sixth of the platform's area and one block about a metre across. Their outline is schematic.
- **What is left out.** The recess down the middle of each face (Petrie estimated 37 inches on the north face; Monnier finds it irregular and over two metres in places), the gash and the entrance on the north face, the dip of the courses (Petrie: up to one degree), the slight turn of the masonry against the casing that Petrie reports and Maragioglio and Rinaldi doubt, and every individual block. The courses are drawn level and whole.
- **The casing still in place.** The 54 m of the lowest course is not drawn in white: at the scale of the model it is 1.5 m of a 138 m pyramid. The film says "almost all".
- **Each step edge has a chamfer** of about 10 cm so that it catches the light. "On the plane" holds to that, not to the tenth of an inch quoted above.
- **The light as two volumes.** The casing is drawn from the pavement to the level of the flat top and the apex above it, so that the film can dim one and keep the other. Together they are one pyramid.

## Known disagreements between sources

- **Number of courses.** 203 (Petrie's plate; the French expedition of 1801; Maragioglio and Rinaldi), 201 (Goyon: 200 to the flat top and one ruined course; Smyth counted 201 going up and 202 coming down), 202 layers (Kawae). The difference is whether the ruined blocks on top count as two courses, one or none, and how many are counted below the flat top (Petrie's levels give 201, Goyon 200; the fact check's own comparison of the two tables puts the difference near Petrie's courses 124 to 129, which is not a published finding). The film says 203 and its legend names Petrie.
- **Present height.** 138.48 m to the highest block by Petrie's levels; 138.745 m by Goyon's sum of step heights. The flat top is at 137.36 to 137.39 m by Petrie's levelling, and Perring's independent "present height, perpendicular" of 1837 (450 ft 9 in, Vyse 1840 II Appendix p. 109) is the same to a quarter of an inch; sums of step heights taken with a rule run higher (Goyon 137.625 m, Kawae 137.9 m). Height lost: 8.2 m above the highest block, 9.3 m above the flat top. The film shows the first pair, 138.5 m and 146.7 m.
- **Original height.** See row 8.
- **Size of the flat top.** See row 2.
- **Date.** See row 6.

## Not said, and why

- **A year or a name for the loss of the casing.** Petrie's "apparently stripped off by Sultan Hasan for his mosque in 1356" is his own hedge ("is said to"), and the pilgrims of 1395 saw stripping still going on. That the earthquake of 1303 shook the casing loose is often repeated, and appears in a recent paper (Hemeda and Sonbol, *Heritage Science* 8:8, 2020) without a source from the time; the standard catalogue of the Arabic reports (Ambraseys, Melville and Adams 1994, pp. 42-43) lists that earthquake's damage without the pyramids.
- **"Tallest structure for more than 3,800 years."** As a flat statement it fails: from the end of Khufu's reign (2566, 2528 or 2483 BC by chronology) to the raising of Lincoln Cathedral's central tower in 1307-1311 is 3,793 to 3,876 years, and the spire's 525 ft is reputed, not measured ("it is said ... but this is doubtful": Kendrick, *The Cathedral Church of Lincoln*, 1898, p. 60). Old St Paul's steeple of 1221 has no recorded height, and reported heights for the Yongning Pagoda (516-534) run both below and above 146.7 m. What stands is the hedged form, "For some 3,800 years, nothing people built is known to have stood taller". The film leaves the claim out; the pinned comment in POST.md uses the hedged form, word for word.
- **Gold on the capstone.** No evidence (row 5). The apex is drawn as plain light.
- **A count of casing stones, or their weight.** No scholarly count was found; the 15-ton figure is for the unusually tall lowest course only.

## Refutation record

14 November 2026, in this order.

1. **Choosing the subject.** Five scouts proposed 19 subjects from different angles; three judges (reach, feasibility, truth and licence) scored them with seven further ideas. The feasibility judge rendered a test model; the truth judge read Petrie's height and summit figures on the scans.
2. **Reading the plate.** As set out under "The course table".
3. **Sourcing and attack.** Eight claims (date and builder, original height, present height and course count, the casing stone, its removal, the capstone, the tallest-structure record, the geometry of the model) were each researched by one agent with web access and then attacked by two others, one as an Egyptologist and one as a pedantic commenter. Changes made as a result:
   - "It stops at a flat top, 138 metres up" was wrong by a metre for the flat top (137.4 m; 138.5 m is the highest block). The line now gives the width of the flat top, and the height is on the readout with its own label.
   - "147 m · 481 ft" does not convert (147 m is 482 ft). The readout now shows one decimal: 146.7 m, 481 ft.
   - "Built smooth" became "finished smooth".
   - "Was taken to build medieval Cairo" became "was quarried away in the Middle Ages, much of it to build Cairo".
   - "The capstone has never been found" became "Its own capstone has never been found", because of the capstone of the satellite pyramid.
   - The heading's "c. 2560 BC" became "c. 2600-2500 BC": 2560 falls inside Khufu's reign in none of the main chronologies.
   - The readout's label used to cross-fade while the number was running, so that for a few frames a number stood beside the wrong label. The label now follows the number, frame for frame, and is hidden whenever the number is running.
   - The two blocks drawn above the flat top were about three times too large in area for what Goyon and Kawae describe, and were made smaller.
   - The tallest-structure line was dropped from the draft description.
4. **Review of the finished film.** A candidate render was reviewed from three sides (the facts as finally worded, the picture, the code), and each finding went to a sceptic who had to confirm it before it counted. No spoken or on-screen statement was found false. Changes made as a result:
   - During the last line the only thing lit is the whole missing top, which read as "the capstone". The line now names the top first ("The top is gone too, and its own capstone has never been found"), and POST.md carries a reply.
   - The finished pyramid, whole and labelled, was on screen for about half a second. It is now held through a beat of silence and the first words of the next line.
   - "White" was on screen in two senses (the legend's white for what stands; the caption's white limestone, drawn in orange). The legend now reads TODAY and LOST, and the label at the reveal reads "smooth, white, pointed".
   - The steps strobed when the camera moved fast. Each frame is now the average of twenty views spread over the time the frame lasts.
   - Two caption changes came a quarter and a seventh of a second after the voice. Chunks are now placed by matching from the end of the sentence, which the review measured on 87 boundaries as accurate to about two hundredths of a second; one chunk gives way to the next on a single frame.
   - Every pause was about 0.6 s longer than the script set, because each clip from the synthesiser ends in rumble too low to hear. This film's clips are trimmed on what can be heard.
   - The feet figure was hard to read against the lit model (about 2:1); both units are now in the text colour on a soft shadow. One frame read "138.5 m 455 ft": the feet are now worked from the metres as printed.
   - The readout dropped back to "138.5 m · still standing" while the casing was still mostly lit; it now drops when the casing is gone. The label used to stand beside the running number during the count; it now waits for the count to land.
   - The credit was readable for about a second. It is shorter, and is also shown under the spoken title.
   - The engraved "100 m" read as "188 m" at phone size in the mono face; it is now in the text face.
   - Three corrections to this file: a page number in Edwards, a note in Abd al-Latif's translator, and a year for Kawae's survey that the source does not give.
   - Not changed: the film ends on its opening framing a quarter-turn round, with the readout still up, so the loop is a plain cut. The documents no longer say otherwise.
5. Sources the checkers could not open, and so not relied on: Lehner and Hawass, *Giza and the Pyramids* (2017); Arnold, *Building in Egypt* (1991); Clarke and Engelbach (1930); Cole's 1925 paper in the original (its figures were read in Engelbach's précis and Borchardt's reprint).
