## Description (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube)

Today the Great Pyramid is a staircase: 203 courses of stone, 138.5 m (454 ft) to its highest block, ending in a flat top. As finished it was smooth white limestone and rose to a point at 146.7 m (481 ft). The white model is what stands today, built from a survey of every single course. The light is what is gone.

What do you think was on top?

## Pinned comment

A capstone was found beside the Great Pyramid in the 1990s. It belongs to the small satellite pyramid at its south-east corner, not to the Great Pyramid, whose own has never been found (Lehner, The Complete Pyramids, pp. 222-223; Hawass 1997). And for some 3,800 years, nothing people built is known to have stood taller.

## Replies to keep ready

These are the corrections the fact check expects in the comments. Each reply is true as written; the sources are in FACTS.md.

- **"It was 146.6 m, not 146.7."** Both are in print. Petrie worked out 5,776 inches, 146.71 m, give or take 18 cm; 146.59 m is the same pyramid at a slightly lower slope, 280 cubits. Both are 481 ft.
- **"It has 201 courses."** 203 by Petrie's levels, which the model uses. Goyon counted 200 to the flat top and one ruined course on it; the difference is how the last few blocks are counted.
- **"The flat top is at 137 m."** Yes: 137.4 m. The 138.5 m on screen is the highest block standing on it.
- **"They found the capstone in 1991."** That one belongs to the satellite pyramid. See the pinned comment.
- **"It had a gold capstone."** There is no evidence for gold on any capstone of that dynasty; the ones that survive are plain limestone.
- **"An earthquake in 1303 shook the casing off."** It is often repeated, but the catalogue of the Arabic reports of that earthquake does not mention the pyramids. In 1395 French pilgrims watched masons still prising the casing off one of the Giza pyramids, stone for the buildings of Cairo.
- **"Wikipedia says 2600 BC."** The heading says c. 2600-2500 BC: published dates for Khufu's reign run from 2633 to 2483 BC.
