## What the film does not claim

- It does not say the same seventeen stones stood then as stand today. Stukeley gives no stone numbers.
- It does not say nothing else changed. Stukeley also describes one upright at the south-west leaning on a stone of the inner circle, and six lying on the ground: 24 of 30 visible. Darvill counts seven fallen and six missing, also 24. Which stone the leaning upright was, and when it came to lie as it does now, is not established from the sources opened.
- The title uses 1740, the year the count was printed. The count itself dates from the 1720s, as the first line says.
- It does not identify any stone on the two engraved views.
