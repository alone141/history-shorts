## What the film does not claim

- It does not say who broke the nose. The one named account (Maqrizi, row 8) is a single later report.
- Al-Maqrizi's account says "face" (وجه), not nose. Only Haarmann (1978) says nose and ears, drawing on the wider tradition and on other chroniclers. The spoken line (row 8) says "face" and does not say nose.
- The plates were engraved by Marcus Tuscher in 1743 and 1744, after Norden died on 22 September 1742. Plate XLV is "Tuscher Hafniae fec. 1743" and plate XLVI is "Tuscher Sculp. 1744". Norden never saw the plates printed. The book appeared in 1755.
- The nose is shown on plate XLVI. The 1737 date is engraved on plate XLV. Plate XLVI has no date.
- The Battle of the Pyramids (21 July 1798) was not fought at the Sphinx. The film does not mention 1798 or any battle. No source puts a shot at the Sphinx.
- The date of Gerome's painting is unresolved. Commons tags one file 1886; another file of the same composition (Yorck Project) is tagged 1867-68. The painting is shown as a picture of the legend, not as evidence.
- It does not say Napoleon never visited the Sphinx, or that his soldiers never fired at it. The claim is only that the nose was already damaged before he was born.
- It does not say Norden's drawing is the first without a nose. Research marks "first" as unsourced.
- It does not say the 1737 date is the book's date. The book is 1755.
- It does not give an exact day for Norden's drawing. None of the sources read gives one.
- It does not use Lehner for a date. Lehner's "about 300 years before Napoleon" does not match Maqrizi's date, so it is not used.

## Hostile commenters: what they could say, and the answer

1. **"Norden's plate came out after he died."** True. Norden died on 22 September 1742. Plate XLV was engraved in 1743 and XLVI in 1744, by Tuscher, who also died before the book appeared. Answer: the film says "Norden drew it". It does not say Norden engraved or printed it.
2. **"The engraver took liberties."** On plate XLVII (the profile, not on screen) a faint outline extends beyond the broken nose, as if a nose were sketched in lightly. Answer: the solid drawing stops at the damaged stump. The film does not say "no trace of a nose".
3. **"The 1755 book is not 1737."** Correct. The 1737 date is on plate XLV only. The 1755 date is on the title page. Answer: the film uses both dates for the thing they describe.
4. **"Which words are Norden's?"** The page-85 sentence is in the book's text. The preface says the Danish society edited the book from Norden's notes. Answer: say "the 1755 book says", not "Norden wrote in 1737".
5. **"Maqrizi is later and says 'face'."** Correct. Answer: the spoken line (row 8) says "face". It does not say the nose was attacked in 1378.
6. **"Pococke's 1743 plate shows a nose."** It does. Pococke's own preface says the head is taken from Le Brun, with some changes, so it is a copy and not an independent view. Answer: the film does not mention it. Research did not look at the plate itself.
7. **"Other earlier pictures have noses"** (de Bruijn 1698, Sandys 1615, Helferich 1579). Answer: scholars usually treat these as inaccurate or imaginative, but research did not find a scholarly citation for that. The film does not say "all earlier drawings are wrong".
8. **"Did Napoleon's soldiers fire at it?"** The sources give no evidence either way. Answer: the film claims only that the nose was already damaged before 1769 (the two plates and the 1755 book). It does not claim that his army never fired at it.
9. **"The painting is from 1886, not 1867."** Research: the two dates are both on Commons. Answer: the film does not give a date for the painting. CREDITS.md says the date is unresolved.
10. **"Is there an 18th-century Ottoman or Arab eyewitness?"** Research did not look for one. Answer: not checked.
