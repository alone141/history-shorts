# FACTS: The Sphinx was buried up to its neck

Every spoken and on-screen claim, its source, and the wording or the measurement
behind it.

Status: drafted 15 November 2026 from topics/sphinx/research.md ("Angle 2" and the
earlier sections) and topics/sphinx/sources.json, with the independent hostile
check of 15 November 2026 applied (see "Hostile check" at the end). Not final until
the film is approved. Rows marked "Open" need a decision from the owner.

## What the film does not claim

- It does not say the sand covered the Sphinx without a break for about 1,500 years. The clearances of 1816 and 1885-86 are in Hassan (pp. 10-23).
- It does not say the Sphinx was buried to the neck for most of its history. No scholar found in research.md says that. The "about 1,500 years" is our count from one sentence of Hassan.
- It does not say the "neck" comes from the Dream Stele of Thutmose IV. The stele says "The sands of the Sanctuary, upon which I am, have reached me" (Hassan p. 196). "Neck" is Lehner's inference from the prince sleeping "in the shadow of the statue's head" (Lehner, about pp. 132-133).
- It does not give a head height, a share of the Sphinx hidden, or a sand level as a measurement. No source found gives a head height. The people in the plates were drawn by the engraver, Tuscher, after Norden's death, and are not measurements.
- It does not say who buried the Sphinx, or when the sand first covered it.
- It does not give one year for the clearance. Baraize (from 1925 or 1926) and Hassan (1936-38) both worked on it; the film names neither.
- It does not date the Matson picture more closely than "between 1934 and 1939".
- It does not use the nose, Napoleon or the Maqrizi material in research.md (sections 1-6). That belongs to the other Sphinx film.

## Hostile check (15 November 2026) and answers

An independent hostile check was made on 15 November 2026. Its points and the answers in research.md are below. The commenter list in research.md (Angle 2, A5) is covered by the same answers.

1. **"About 1,500 years is made up."** It is our arithmetic from Hassan p. 9 (4th century AD to the modern clearances). Hassan gives no figure. The POST description says so.
2. **"The sand was cleared in 1816 and 1885-86, so it was not buried for 1,500 years."** Correct. Hassan pp. 10-23 and Lehner describe both. The film says "remained until recent times", Hassan's words.
3. **"Norden's 1737 plates show no neck."** Wrong. Norden's plates show the head and neck, and XLVI shows a stepped block of upper chest, above the sand (research.md A1).
4. **"The 1737 date is not on the plate on screen."** Correct. The date is on plate XLV only. XLVI reads 1744 and XLVII has no year. "Drew it in 1737" refers to Norden's stay at Cairo, July to November 1737 (vol. 1 preface).
5. **"The Teynard print is 1853-54."** The Met dates the photograph 1851-52 and the print 1853-54. The film says "early 1850s".
6. **"73 m is the body only."** It is the whole monument, paws to tail (Rigano 2014 p. 148, as cited in Wikipedia).
7. **"The Sphinx was not buried in antiquity, so 'fully dug out again' is wrong."** Thutmose IV cleared it about 1400 BC and the Romans repaired it (Hassan pp. 8-9; Lehner). "Again" is right.
8. **"The Matson picture is from 1936."** The Library of Congress gives "between 1934 and 1939" for matpc.17957. Do not caption a single year.
9. **"The stele says 'neck'."** It does not. It says "The sands ... have reached me". "Neck" is Lehner's inference.
10. **"Sources differ on the sand line: shoulders or neck?"** Yes. Hassan says "to the neck" and NOVA "to its neck"; Smithsonian and Wikipedia say "to its shoulders" (research.md A5, 4). The film uses Hassan's word and shows the chest in the pictures.
11. **"The clearance dates differ."** Yes. Caviglia 1816 or 1817; Baraize 1925 or 1926, ending 1934, 1936 or 1937. The film uses "1930s" and "from 1925 or 1926" is not on screen.
12. **"Medieval accounts are first-hand."** No. They are second-hand, as quoted by Hassan. The film does not use them.
