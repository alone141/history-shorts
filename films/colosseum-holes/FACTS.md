# FACTS: Why is the Colosseum full of holes?

Every spoken and on-screen claim, its source, and the wording or the method
behind it.

Status: drafted 17 November 2026 from topics/colosseum_holes.py and
topics/colosseum/ (research.md, pictures.md, sources.json, and factcheck.md,
including its "Checks of the final wording" section of 16 and 17 November 2026).

## What the film does not claim

- It does not count the holes or the clamps. No source gives a number.
- It does not say who dug the clamps out, or in which century. The sources give "post-Roman", "the early Middle Ages" (Middleton) and, on one website, "probably during the Gothic War". The film says only "After Rome fell".
- It does not say barbarians or Goths made the holes. Line 4 asks whether it was barbarians or market stalls, as the 1651 page did, and does not answer.
- It does not say every hole is a clamp hole. Original lifting holes, pin holes at a repair, marble-fixture clamp holes on inner walls and modern ties exist (research.md section 5). "Most" is the film's word.
- It does not say the metal is iron by analysis. No analysis of a surviving clamp or of the metal in a socket was found.
- It does not say the 300 tons were removed or stolen. The figure is metal used in building, and it is one estimate with no named author.
- It does not give a clamp's shape, size or count, or the mortar detail. It does not claim the clamps were set in lead as its own statement; the underlined Middleton line on screen says "run with lead", which is Middleton's word (row 20).
- It does not say the holes caused any collapse.

## What could not be checked

- The standard modern books. Claridge, Hopkins and Beard, Lancaster (the clamps chapter is behind a paywall), Rea, Cozzo, Lugli, Gabucci and Coarelli were not opened: they are lending-only on archive.org or not online. The Parco's pages on clamps were not opened either. Where a claim rests on one of them, research.md marks it "via" a source that was read.
- Who estimated the 300 tons. Nobody is named as its author. Art of Making gives none; the-colosseum.net says "calculated" and cites Cozzo (1971) as its main source without attributing the sentence; Wikipedia's sentence cites Claridge (1998), which was not read. No calculation (blocks, joints, clamp size) was found.
- Any analysis of surviving metal. "Iron" is the general word in the sources. "Set in lead" rests on one thesis excerpt (Manieri Elia) and on general Roman practice in Art of Making, tools/18; no Colosseum socket has been shown to contain lead.
- Dated evidence for the digging. No source dates the clamp removal. The Parco's excavation dates the robbing of the floor stone to the 11th-13th centuries (research.md section 3), not the clamps.
- The author's name. The notes spell it three ways (Joseph-Marie Suarès; Joseph Marie Suares; Giuseppe Maria Suarès). The archive record and the bishop of Vaison identification match; the spelling needs one choice before any caption or credit uses it. The posts use "Suaresius".
- The Suaresius title-page imprint. The record date is 1651 and the Lyon reprint is 1652; the OCR did not show the imprint, so the place and year of the first edition are the archive's.
- The Met's licence pages. The record pages answered 429 to curl; CC0 was read from the Met collection API's isPublicDomain flag.

## Hostile check (16 and 17 November 2026)

A hostile read was made on 16 November 2026, and three further checks on the later wordings (16 and 17 November). The full verdicts are in topics/colosseum/factcheck.md. The three likeliest corrections, and what the film does with each:

1. **"About 300 tons" is the metal taken out.** The figure is unattributed, says tonnes, and is metal used in building. After the digging lines, a viewer may hear "removed". The film now says "METAL USED TO BUILD IT" on the plate, names Art of Making as the source of the figure, and says "by one estimate" in the voice. Left as it is: the spoken "tons" for the source's "tonnes".
2. **"Iron" and "After Rome fell" are stated as facts.** No analysis of surviving metal was found, and no source dates the digging. Middleton says "early Middle Ages"; Art of Making says "post-Roman". The film keeps "It's right here: iron clamps tied each block to the next." and "After Rome fell, iron was valuable," (true but challengeable). It gives no century and names no one.
3. **"The holes never left."** The check called "never left" rhetoric: restorations have filled or changed some holes. "Most" is the only hedge. The line is kept as the owner accepted it, and is true but unsourced.

## Script revisions (16 and 17 November 2026)

- "So what do historians say?" became "So what's the answer today?" (row 5).
- "this page blamed barbarians" was dropped. Page 4 gives the barbarian story and then prefers market-stall poles, so the line now asks the question: "In 1651, this page asked: was it barbarians, or market stalls?" (row 4).
- The plate source changed from "Middleton 1892 · Suaresius 1651 · The Met" to "Figure: Art of Making, King's College London" (row 17).
- The loupe changed from 3x to 2x on 17 November 2026 (row 23).
- Spoken forms: "sixteen fifty-one" for "1651", and "three hundred" for "300". The caption keeps the numerals.
